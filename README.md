Curated List of SaaS Products & Open-Source GitHub Projects
Focused on Quality Management, Document Control, CAPA, Audits, Nonconformances, Training, Supplier Quality & Regulatory Compliance
Last updated: August 2026

This repository tracks notable SaaS/hosted platforms and open-source projects for Quality Management Systems (QMS). These tools help organizations manage controlled documents, SOPs, training, audits, nonconformances, CAPA, change control, complaints, supplier quality, risk management, inspections, quality events, electronic approvals, and audit trails.

Examples include MasterControl, Qualio, ETQ Reliance, Greenlight Guru, Arena QMS, QT9 QMS, Intelex QMS, Sparta TrackWise, AssurX, and Qualis.

Open-source emphasis: This section is heavily expanded with open-source QMS platforms, electronic QMS (eQMS) projects, manufacturing-quality systems, ISO 9001 frameworks, medical-device quality tooling, CAPA systems, document-control frameworks, audit-management projects, and reusable quality-workflow building blocks. The open-source ecosystem is smaller than the commercial QMS market, but projects such as OpenQMS, Open QMS, QAtrial, QMSCAPA, qms-kit, and other GitHub-native QMS implementations provide increasingly useful foundations for self-hosted quality systems.

Open-Source GitHub Projects

OpenQMS — Open-Industry-System
Full-stack open-source QMS for manufacturing with FMEA, 8D/CAPA, control plans, SPC, MSA, incoming quality control, supplier management, SCAR, customer complaints, APQP, PPAP, audits, management review, and quality objectives. It is designed around IATF 16949-oriented manufacturing quality processes and is released under the MIT License.

Open QMS — IridiumSoftware
GitHub-native open-source QMS generator that creates quality-management scaffolding based on products, jurisdictions, standards, and modules. It uses GitHub pull requests, issues, Actions, and Git history to provide controlled-document workflows and traceability.

QAtrial
Open-source AI-powered quality-management platform for regulated industries including pharmaceutical, medical-device, biotech, CRO, and GAMP-oriented environments. Modules include audit trails, electronic signatures, change control, CAPA, deviations, training, supplier qualification, complaints, risk management, document control, validation, and traceability.

qms-kit
Reusable ISO 9001 QMS deployment kit that scaffolds documentation structures, procedures, management-review materials, internal audits, CAPA/nonconformance trackers, and KPI dashboards across self-hosted XWiki/Redmine, Atlassian, or Microsoft 365 environments.

QMS — senrecep
Full-featured open-source QMS implementation centered around structured document management, multi-level approvals, controlled distribution, auditable read confirmations, corrective-action tracking, authentication, roles, and ISO 9001-oriented workflows.

DearAuditor Open eQMS
GitHub-native open eQMS baseline supporting ISO 9001, ISO 13485, ISO 14971, IEC 62304, IEC 62366-1, ISO/IEC 27001, GAMP 5, and other regulated workflows. It uses Git repositories, issues, pull requests, Actions, and releases as the quality-system infrastructure.

QMSCAPA
Free QMS/CAPA software providing capabilities for nonconformance and corrective actions, controlled documents, electronic approval, customer feedback, complaints, RMAs, recalls, supplier evaluations, risk assessments, objectives, process monitoring, and root-cause analysis.

QMS MCP Server
Open-source MCP-based quality-management server providing tools for suppliers, batches, QC release, inspections, nonconformances, CAPA, complaints, and HACCP monitoring. Useful as an AI-agent integration layer rather than a conventional end-user eQMS.

Important distinction: The open-source QMS ecosystem is considerably smaller and more fragmented than the commercial ecosystem represented by MasterControl, ETQ Reliance, TrackWise, Greenlight Guru, Arena QMS, Qualio, and Intelex. Some open projects are complete applications, while others are QMS frameworks, GitHub-native compliance systems, deployment kits, or specialized quality modules rather than direct one-for-one replacements.

The strongest open-source strategy is therefore to assemble a modular QMS around:

Document Control → Quality Events → Nonconformance → CAPA → Change Control → Risk → Audit → Training → Supplier Quality → Complaints → Management Review → Analytics

A practical self-hosted architecture can combine:

OpenQMS / QAtrial / Open QMS + PostgreSQL + Keycloak + MinIO + Git + GitHub/GitLab + Grafana

with dedicated modules for:

Document Control — controlled documents, revision history, approval workflows, obsolete-document management, and point-of-use distribution.

SOP Management — policies, procedures, work instructions, forms, and controlled templates.

Electronic Signatures — approval/signature workflows and signer identity.

Audit Trails — immutable or append-only records of important quality-system events.

CAPA — corrective and preventive action initiation, investigation, root-cause analysis, action assignment, verification, and closure.

Nonconformance — quality-event intake, containment, investigation, disposition, and corrective action.

Change Control — controlled changes to documents, processes, products, equipment, suppliers, and systems.

Deviation Management — investigation and disposition of deviations from approved procedures or specifications.

Risk Management — FMEA, risk registers, hazard analysis, risk controls, and mitigation tracking.

Internal Audits — audit planning, checklists, findings, corrective actions, and closure evidence.

Supplier Quality — supplier qualification, evaluations, audits, scorecards, SCARs, and supplier corrective actions.

Complaint Management — customer complaints, investigation, escalation, and linkage to CAPA.

Training Management — role-based training assignments, competency tracking, and training records.

Quality Objectives — KPIs, targets, management-review inputs, and continuous-improvement metrics.

Inspection & Testing — incoming inspection, in-process inspection, final inspection, sampling plans, and quality results.

SPC — control charts, process capability, statistical rules, and trend detection.

FMEA — process/product risk analysis and linkage to control plans.

APQP / PPAP — manufacturing quality planning and production-part approval workflows.

Management Review — structured management-review agendas, metrics, decisions, and action tracking.

Traceability — linking requirements → risks → documents → tests → changes → approvals → quality events.

Analytics — CAPA aging, audit findings, defect rates, supplier performance, complaint trends, training completion, and quality KPIs.

A strong self-hosted QMS architecture can therefore look like:

Users → Identity/RBAC → QMS Web App → Workflow Engine → PostgreSQL → Object Storage → Audit/Event Store → Analytics

with Git/GitHub/GitLab providing an especially interesting foundation for document versioning, approvals, traceability, and audit history in Git-native QMS implementations.

For a more sophisticated MasterControl/ETQ/TrackWise-style platform, the architecture can be extended to:

Quality Event → Investigation → Root Cause → Risk Assessment → CAPA → Verification → Approval → Effectiveness Check → Closure → Management Review

and connected to:

ERP + MES + PLM + LIMS + CRM + Supplier Portal + Training System + Data Warehouse

This creates a highly modular open-source QMS ecosystem capable of supporting manufacturing, medical devices, pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, food manufacturing, aerospace, automotive, and other regulated environments.

Disclaimer

This is a community-curated list — not exhaustive and not an endorsement.

Dedicated open-source enterprise QMS platforms remain considerably less mature and less numerous than commercial eQMS products.

Some projects listed here are complete QMS applications, while others are frameworks, deployment kits, compliance baselines, specialized modules, or integration layers.

An open-source QMS does not automatically make an organization ISO/FDA/GxP compliant. Processes, validation, governance, security, electronic signatures, records retention, and operating procedures must be appropriately implemented and validated.

Regulated organizations should perform their own computer-system validation, supplier qualification, risk assessment, and regulatory assessment before using an open-source QMS in production.

Standards and regulatory requirements change over time. Always verify the applicable current edition and jurisdictional requirements.

Licensing varies by project. Verify the current license and commercial-use requirements before deployment.
